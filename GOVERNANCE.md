# Project Governance

## Purpose

This document describes how the Open Journalism Evidence Kit (OJEK) is maintained and how community participation can influence its direction. It is a starting framework and should evolve through public review.

## Independence

OJEK is infrastructure, not a newsroom. It does not appoint a universal editor, certify truth, or control the editorial decisions of participating publishers and communities.

Each independent node, newsroom, archive, and contributor retains responsibility for its own publication, collection, retention, moderation, and legal obligations.

## Roles

### Contributors
Anyone may propose changes, report problems, contribute translations, review sources, or participate in discussions under the project policies.

### Reviewers
Reviewers provide subject-matter, language, technical, accessibility, security, or community-context review. A review is evidence of review—not a guarantee of correctness.

### Maintainers
Maintainers are responsible for repository health, release management, review coordination, and enforcing project policies. Maintainer status does not grant editorial authority over independent publishers or nodes.

### Project stewards
Stewards help resolve governance questions, conflicts of interest, and policy disputes. Stewardship should be transparent and periodically reviewed.

## Decision process

- Small, low-risk changes may be accepted by maintainers after review.
- Changes to schemas, trust assumptions, privacy policy, rights policy, or federation behavior require a written proposal and broader review.
- Significant proposals should describe the problem, alternatives, affected groups, risks, migration impact, and test plan.
- Where a change materially affects a region or language community, seek review from contributors with relevant local knowledge.
- Record material decisions and reasons in repository history or a decision log.
- Prefer consensus. If consensus is not possible, maintainers may make a documented decision with dissent and unresolved risks recorded.
- Emergency security or safety changes may be applied quickly, followed by a public explanation that does not expose sensitive details.

## Conflicts of interest

Contributors and maintainers should disclose relevant financial, organizational, political, or personal interests when those interests could reasonably affect a decision. Disclosures should be proportionate and should not require unnecessary personal information.

Funding does not purchase editorial control, favorable ranking, data access, or preferential treatment.

## Project assets and data

- Public records and schemas should use documented open formats.
- The project should not collect more personal data than necessary.
- Sensitive source material does not belong in public project repositories.
- No node is required to replicate another node's content.
- Retention, restriction, and removal processes must remain possible.
- Project infrastructure should be replaceable and exportable.

## Releases

A release should identify:
- Version and source commit.
- Changes and compatibility impact.
- Tests and validation performed.
- Known limitations and unresolved risks.
- Migration or rollback guidance where applicable.

A document describing a capability is not proof that the capability has been implemented.

## Amendments

Anyone may propose a governance change through an issue or pull request. Material changes should include a rationale and allow time for community review. The current version remains in force until the change is accepted and recorded.
