# Training Exercise: The Northbridge Library Contract

**Synthetic scenario only.** Northbridge, its institutions, vendors, people, documents, dates, and amounts in this exercise are fictional. They do not describe real entities or allegations.

## Learning objectives
- Turn a broad concern into a testable public-interest question.
- Build a source register and claim-evidence matrix.
- Distinguish primary records from commentary.
- Detect circular sourcing and missing context.
- Record uncertainty and identify what cannot yet be concluded.
- Practice correction and right-of-reply workflows.

## Scenario
A fictional community newsletter reports that the fictional Northbridge City Council awarded a five-year library digitization contract to a vendor called PaperBridge. The newsletter says the award was "secret" and "three times over budget." A council agenda, a public award notice, and a budget spreadsheet are available in the training packet (facilitators may create these mock records locally). No real personal data should be used.

## Assignment question
What can the public records establish about the procurement process, award value, and stated reasons for selecting the vendor?

Do not begin by assuming wrongdoing. Consider ordinary explanations such as contract scope changes, optional renewals, tax treatment, or differences between estimated and awarded values.

## Mock source list
Create these fictional documents for the exercise or replace them with locally generated mock files:
- NBR-01: Council agenda dated 10 March 2025, listing a digitization procurement item.
- NBR-02: Public award notice dated 22 March 2025, naming PaperBridge and a base contract value of 240,000 fictional units.
- NBR-03: Budget spreadsheet showing 720,000 fictional units over five years, including two optional renewal periods and support services.
- NBR-04: Newsletter post claiming the contract was secret and three times over budget.
- NBR-05: Mock council minutes explaining the procurement timeline and approval vote.

All values and identifiers are invented for instruction. They are not evidence about any real procurement.

## Participant tasks
1. Write a one-sentence investigation question and define the date range.
2. Identify which mock records are primary records and which are secondary commentary.
3. Populate the source register template.
4. Create at least three claims in the claim-evidence matrix:
   - Whether an award notice was publicly issued.
   - What the base contract value was.
   - Whether the 720,000 figure includes optional periods or additional services.
5. Identify whether NBR-04's wording is supported, contradicted, or incomplete.
6. List at least two alternative explanations and one missing record that would help resolve uncertainty.
7. Draft a fair, neutral question to the fictional procurement office.
8. Complete the pre-publication checklist and state what you would not publish yet.

## Facilitator notes / expected reasoning
- A public award notice and agenda would weigh against the unqualified claim that no public notice existed, but the exercise must distinguish publication timing, discoverability, and legal sufficiency.
- A 240,000 base value and 720,000 multi-year maximum are not necessarily contradictory if the latter includes optional renewals and support.
- Do not infer favoritism, corruption, or intent from the difference alone.
- A complete contract, evaluation criteria, amendments, and payment records may be needed to answer different questions.
- The newsletter is a lead and should be checked against the underlying records.
- If a mock source is later corrected, preserve the original training record and add a correction notice.

## Debrief questions
- Which claim was easiest to verify, and why?
- Which sources were not independent?
- What did the records not establish?
- What context would a reader need?
- How could the reporting harm a person or community if published carelessly?
- What would change if the documents were incomplete, translated, or obtained under a public-records law?

## Assessment rubric
| Dimension | Strong performance |
|---|---|
| Question and scope | Narrow, answerable, public-interest purpose stated |
| Source quality | Primary sources prioritized; source dependencies noted |
| Verification | Claims trace to specific records and dates |
| Uncertainty | Missing evidence and alternatives are explicit |
| Fairness | No unsupported motive or wrongdoing claim |
| Safety and rights | No unnecessary personal data; rights and context considered |
| Reproducibility | Another participant can repeat the checks |
