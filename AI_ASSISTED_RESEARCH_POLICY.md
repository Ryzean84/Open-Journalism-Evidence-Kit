# AI-Assisted Research and Editorial Accountability Policy

**Draft policy v0.1**

## Purpose
Permit carefully bounded uses of automation while protecting accuracy, privacy, source confidentiality, and editorial independence.

## Permitted assistance (with human review)
- Organizing public-source metadata.
- Suggesting search terms or research leads.
- Extracting text from public documents for review.
- Drafting summaries that are checked against the original.
- Translation drafts reviewed by a competent human.
- Detecting duplicate records or inconsistent metadata.
- Helping generate tests, documentation, and reproducible scripts.

## Prohibited reliance
- AI output is never a source or proof.
- Do not publish generated quotations, citations, legal interpretations, identities, or factual claims without checking the primary material.
- Do not let automated scores decide credibility, guilt, intent, or whether a person should be investigated.
- Do not upload confidential source identities, private communications, credentials, or sensitive unpublished material to an external model without explicit organizational approval and a documented privacy/security assessment.
- Do not use synthetic media or fabricated personas as evidence.

## Required disclosure
For material AI use, record:
- Tool and model name/version where available.
- Date of use.
- Task performed.
- Source material supplied, described at a safe level without exposing protected data.
- Human verification steps.
- Known limitations and errors found.

Disclosure to readers should be proportionate and clear when AI materially affected reporting, analysis, translation, or presentation.

## Editorial accountability
A named human editor remains accountable for each published factual claim. AI-generated summaries must be compared with original sources. Translation must be labeled as a translation and must not replace the original. Researchers must actively test for hallucinations, omitted context, fabricated citations, and automation bias.

## Review and change
Review this policy at least annually and after any serious incident involving source exposure, fabricated evidence, or materially incorrect AI output.
