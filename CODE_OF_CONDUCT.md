# Community Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

The Open Journalism Evidence Kit is intended for a global community with different languages, cultures, experiences, political views, and levels of technical expertise. We are committed to a respectful, accessible, and safety-conscious environment.

Participation is open to people working in journalism, research, archiving, education, technology, public-interest law, and local communities.

## Expected behavior

- Treat people with dignity and respect.
- Critique ideas, evidence, and methods—not a person's identity or worth.
- Be curious about local context and cultural differences.
- Make room for contributors who are new, non-technical, or working in languages other than English.
- Disclose conflicts of interest and relevant affiliations.
- Attribute other people's work.
- Accept good-faith correction and explain disagreements with evidence.
- Prioritize the safety of sources, contributors, and affected communities.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, threats, intimidation, stalking, or targeted abuse.
- Racist, sexist, xenophobic, homophobic, transphobic, or otherwise discriminatory conduct.
- Doxxing or publishing private information without a lawful and appropriate basis.
- Pressuring people to reveal confidential sources or private investigative material.
- Impersonation, fabricated evidence, deliberate source misrepresentation, or knowingly deceptive attribution.
- Spam, coordinated disruption, or attempts to manipulate project decisions through harassment.
- Retaliation against people who raise safety, ethics, or quality concerns.

## Editorial disagreement

The project does not require political or editorial uniformity. Disagreement is expected. Claims should be supported by evidence, uncertainty should be visible, and competing interpretations may remain unresolved. No contributor receives authority to declare a disputed claim true merely by status or seniority.

## Reporting concerns

Do not post sensitive allegations, private source identities, credentials, or security details in a public issue. Report conduct concerns privately to project maintainers through GitHub's private contact or reporting mechanisms. Include only the information needed to understand the concern.

Maintainers should review reports promptly, limit access to sensitive information, avoid retaliation, and communicate any action that can safely be disclosed. Where a maintainer is involved in a report, the concern should be routed to another project steward where possible.

## Enforcement

Depending on severity and context, maintainers may issue a reminder, request an apology or correction, temporarily restrict participation, remove harmful content, or exclude a person from project spaces. Decisions should be proportionate, documented, and open to review where practical.

This code applies to project repositories, issues, pull requests, discussions, events, and spaces represented as official project channels.
