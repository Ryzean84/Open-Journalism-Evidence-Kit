# Rights, Copyright, and Ethical Preservation Policy

## Core rule
The project preserves evidence and access pathways to public information. It does not assume that public availability grants permission to copy, mirror, translate, or republish the underlying work.

## Default collection policy
Prefer:
- canonical source URL;
- title, publisher, byline, dates, and language;
- retrieval timestamp and collection method;
- rights and license metadata;
- content digest;
- permitted short quotations or factual metadata;
- links to publisher-hosted versions and authorized archives.

Do not automatically copy full articles, photographs, video, audio, paywalled content, databases, or user-generated content into a public mirror.

## Full-content preservation
Only consider full-content storage when there is a documented basis, such as explicit permission from the rights holder; an applicable open license and compliance with its conditions; public-domain status verified for the relevant jurisdiction; a specific legal exception reviewed for the intended use and territory; or an agreement with a publisher, library, or archive.

Record the basis, scope, territory, term, attribution, and any removal or embargo requirements. Cross-border copyright rules can differ; obtain qualified advice for uncertain cases.

## Privacy and source protection
- Do not publish source identities, private communications, credentials, precise location data, or sensitive personal information by default.
- Do not use the archive to facilitate doxxing, harassment, stalking, intimidation, or targeting.
- Apply special caution to minors, victims, witnesses, refugees, dissidents, and vulnerable communities.
- Support redaction and restricted access for sensitive material.
- Do not place sensitive data or identifying hashes into public immutable logs without review.

## Corrections and removal
The system must support correction, restriction, and removal workflows. An append-only history should record the existence of a correction without unnecessarily retaining the harmful content or revealing protected identities. Replicas must receive restriction notices, but the project must not promise universal deletion from independently operated nodes.

## Editorial integrity
Separate source material, factual metadata, analyst observations, machine-generated summaries, translations, and editorial conclusions.

Every derived claim should point to its evidence and disclose uncertainty. Automated outputs are aids, not editorial decisions.
